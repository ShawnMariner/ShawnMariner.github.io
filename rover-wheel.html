PROJECT OVERVIEW

Designing mobility for loose lunar terrain.

This team project investigated different mobility systems for small GREMLINS microrovers intended to operate on loose, regolith-like terrain.

The research compared wheel and tread concepts while also evaluating multiple grouser geometries to understand how mobility design affects traction, slippage, climbing ability, and speed.

ROLE Mechanical Design & Test Engineer

02

ENGINEERING PROBLEM

Which mobility system performs best?

The team compared small rover wheels and treads and investigated how different grouser shapes influenced performance on loose granular terrain.

The goal was to identify mobility configurations that could provide effective traction and stability while keeping the rover system lightweight and compact.

03

DESIGN & PROTOTYPING

Build, test, analyze, redesign.

The team used an iterative development process to move from mobility concepts into physical prototypes.

Wheel and tread designs were modeled in CAD and 3D printed for testing. Multiple grouser geometries were explored to compare how tread shape affected interaction with the terrain.

TESTING

Evaluating performance in granular terrain.

Prototype mobility systems were tested in a sandbox environment under different slope angles and rover loads.

The team evaluated traction, slippage, climb success, and average speed while documenting performance using measurements, photos, video, and graphs.

04

MY CONTRIBUTION

Mechanical design, prototyping, and testing.

I contributed to concept selection, CAD modeling in Fusion 360, rapid prototyping, and the production of 3D-printed wheel designs.

I also participated in rover traction and slip testing in simulated lunar terrain and supported the team's design iteration process through physical testing and observation.

05

PRELIMINARY FINDINGS

Different terrain conditions favored different designs.

Preliminary testing indicated that wheels performed slightly better on moderate slopes, while treaded designs performed well in very loose sand.

The project also investigated whether Chevron-style grousers would provide superior traction and stability compared with other tested configurations.
