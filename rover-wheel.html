01

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Designing mobility for loose lunar terrain.

This team project investigated different mobility systems for GREMLINS micro-rovers intended to operate on rough, loose, regolith-like terrain.

The team compared the rover's installed treads with alternative wheel concepts and evaluated different grouser geometries to study traction, slippage, climbing performance, distance traveled, and speed.

02

ENGINEERING PROBLEM

Which mobility design performs best?

The team compared treads and wheel-based mobility concepts to understand which configurations provided better locomotion on loose terrain under different slope and loading conditions.

A major design variable was grouser geometry. The project examined how different wheel surface patterns affected grip, stability, slippage, and climbing performance.

03

DESIGN & PROTOTYPING

Build, test, analyze, redesign.

The project used an iterative design process that moved from mobility concepts into CAD models and physical prototypes.

Grouser designs were assigned across the team, modeled in CAD, and 3D printed as wheel prototypes for sandbox testing.

TESTING

Evaluating performance in granular terrain.

The team first conducted control testing using the rover's existing treads, then installed prototype wheels and tested different configurations in a sandbox environment.

Testing compared mobility performance across different rover weights and incline angles while evaluating traction, slippage, climb success, and average speed.

04

MY CONTRIBUTION

Mechanical design, prototyping, and testing.

I contributed to concept selection, CAD modeling in Fusion 360, rapid prototyping, and the development of 3D-printed wheel designs.

I also participated in traction and slip testing in the rover sandbox environment and supported the team's iterative process of building, testing, analyzing, and redesigning.

05

FINDINGS

Performance depended on terrain and loading.

Testing showed that different mobility configurations performed better under different combinations of rover weight and incline.

Preliminary results showed that wheels performed slightly better on moderate slopes, while treads performed well in looser sand. Based on the team's overall data analysis, the Straight grouser was selected as the best overall grouser design.
